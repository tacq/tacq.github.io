Kerf-Bent Plywood Display Shelf

My first experiment in bending rigid plywood into a continuous mid-century modern capsule shelf. Designed from scratch, kerf-cut across all four corners so the plywood wraps smoothly without steaming, joined to custom splayed legs with wooden dowels, and finished by hand with a painted oak wood-grain texture.

Overview & design goal

Standard plywood is strong and dimensionally stable, but its cross-laminated veneers resist bending. For this woodworking project, I wanted to challenge myself to build a mid-century modern capsule display shelf with sweeping, continuous rounded corners — my very first time attempting to bend plywood.

Instead of using steam boxes or thin flexible veneers over heavy forms, I used kerf bending: cutting a series of precisely spaced relief slots across the inside face of the plywood down to the final outer veneer layer, allowing a thick multi-ply board to curve cleanly around a tight radius while retaining structural rigidity once glued.

From raw plywood to finished piece

🪚

1. Kerf-cutting & bending the loop

Calculated the arc length for each 90° corner and cut evenly spaced kerf relief slots on the inner face of the plywood. Glue was worked into the kerfs before clamping the upper and lower shells around the horizontal center deck and vertical divider.

📐

2. Splayed legs & dowel joinery

Cut four matching parallelogram legs from laminated plywood, scribed diagonal centerlines on each end, and drilled centered mortises for fluted wooden dowels to lock the legs into the underside without visible screws.

🎨

3. Edge filling & hand-painted grain

Filled the exposed kerf slots and plywood laminations, sanded the curved corners flush, and hand-painted the entire surface and edges with a warm natural oak wood-grain pattern sealed under a gloss topcoat.

🏎️

4. Multi-tier display layout

The upper full-width bay fits wide scale models (such as a 1:8 supercar), the flat top holds a perpetual calendar, and the two lower divided cubbies organize smaller die-cast and figure collectibles.
